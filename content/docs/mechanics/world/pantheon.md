The peoples of every world worship gods. The gods are part of the history: they are chosen when the world is made,
they gain and lose followers over the centuries, and their temples and shrines are still standing when play begins.

Whether the gods are real is never said. The townsfolk believe in them, and that is enough to shape the world.

# Domains and Names

A god is a **domain** with a **name**. The domains are a fixed catalogue, the same in every world. The names are new
in every world. So every world may have a god of war, but in one world she is Varaska of the Red Spear and in the
next he is Old Durm.

A world does not know every domain:

| World size              | Gods  |
| ----------------------- | ----- |
| Small (like the genesis world, 128 km) | 4 to 5 |
| Large                   | about 10 |

The land picks most of them. A world with long coasts likely knows a god of the sea, a world of rich farmland a god
of the harvest, a world of volcanoes and blight a god of death or of healing. A god can also come into worship later,
when the history gives a reason: a people at war for a century turns to the god of war, a plague turns people to the
god of healing.

## One God, Many Names

Each people calls a god by a name in its own language. Two peoples who worship the same goddess call her by two
names, and both know it. A scholar might say the Boshold's Erzmutter is the coastal people's Ilvarren. The chronicle
always knows they are one god.

**Sacred bestia** is the [bestia kind](/docs/mechanics/bestias/bestia-design/) a god's followers honour. A town
whose patron holds a kind sacred will not hunt it, and its temple shows it in stone.

**Leans toward** is which of the three [Orders](/docs/mechanics/society/factions/) the god's followers find easiest to
believe. It is a hint, not a rule. On the first world, where the Orders are unknown, it only colours what the
faithful say. On later worlds a cult of a god that leans toward an Order is more likely to join it.

# What Worship Leaves Behind

Gods are worshipped in three ways, and each leaves something in the world:

- **Temples.** Each people and each town has a **patron god**. The town's temple is dedicated to it, and its priests
  speak of it. A temple outlives its town: a ruined temple is the most common sign of a god in a ruin.
- **Shrines and sanctuaries.** Holy places outside the towns: an idol in a grove, a stone circle on a hill, a cairn on
  a headland. Many belonged to a cult that is gone.
- **Customs.** Things the townsfolk still do without knowing why: a bestia nobody hunts, a festival, a name day, a
  field left fallow.

Each domain leaves its own traces:

| Death            | Ossuaries beside the cemetery, black-earth shrines at tombs                  |
| Sun              | Gilded discs on gates, sun stones on hilltops                                 |
| Moon             | Silver offerings in pools, hare figures                                       |

# Cults

A cult is smaller than a temple's faith. It may worship one of the world's gods, a kind of bestia, or the star that
fell. A prophet founds it. It spreads from town to town along the roads. It can be persecuted by the rulers, rise in
revolt, and take towns for itself. It can die out, or, on a world where the Orders are known, join one of them. The
[World History](/docs/mechanics/world/world-history/#gods-and-cults) page describes what cults do in history.

# Quests for the Gods

{{< alert context="warning" text="**Design only.** There is no quest system yet (see [Questing](/docs/mechanics/world/questing/))." />}}

The gods are a steady source of small quests. Any temple or shrine can ask for an **offering**, and each domain asks
for **deeds** of its own. These quests are personal: every player can take them, and they come back, so they never run
out.

| Domain           | Quest ideas                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any              | Make an offering at the local shrine; restore a ruined shrine                                    |
| War              | Clear the restless dead from an old battlefield; return a fallen warrior's blade to the temple   |
| Love             | Carry a letter between two towns through the [postal system](/docs/mechanics/towns-trade/postal/) |
| Death            | Lay bones found in the wild to rest in a tomb; tend a forgotten grave                           |
| Sun              | Light the signal fire on a hilltop at dawn                                                       |
| Moon             | Make an offering at a holy pool on a full-moon night                                             |
| Sky and thunder  | Bring a stone struck by lightning                                                                |
| Sea              | Offer a rare catch at the headland shrine                                                        |
| Harvest          | Bring the first crops of the season                                                              |
| Hearth and home  | Bring firewood to keep the temple fire alight                                                    |
| Wisdom           | Bring a rubbing of an old inscription                                                            |
| Craft            | Offer a crafted piece of good quality                                                            |
| Trade and travel | Deliver goods between two towns; repair a milestone                                              |
| Trickery         | Play a harmless trick on a townsperson, without being caught                                     |
| Healing          | Bring healing herbs to the sick                                                                  |
| The hunt         | Hunt the predator that threatens the sacred beast                                                |
| Fate             | Ask the oracle, and do what it says                                                              |
| Justice          | Return stolen goods to their owner                                                               |
| Revelry          | Bring wine or ale for the festival                                                               |
| Night and dreams | Sleep at the shrine and tell the priest the dream                                                |

A god rewards with a small **blessing** (a short buff in the god's domain), standing with the town, and sometimes an
item. Doing many deeds for one god may earn a lasting title.
