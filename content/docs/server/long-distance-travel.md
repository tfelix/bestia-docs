A **journey** is an order to travel through waypoints in turn; the last one is the destination. It is
carried out on the server, so it goes on when the player looks at another entity, logs out, or orders it from
the [companion app]({{< ref "mechanics/companion-app" >}}). A player's click on the map, a saved route sent
from the app and a creature's own AI all start the same thing.

| Who orders it                  | Entity                     | How                                              |
|--------------------------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| A player, in the game          | the master, any bestia     | `MoveEntityCMSG` with waypoints                  |
| A player, in the companion app | a bestia in the world      | `PUT .../bestias/{id}/journey` over the bus      |
| A creature's AI                | any NPC                    | the `TravelTo` behaviour tree leaf               |

The code is the `travel` slice in `zone-server`, between `casting` and `ai`.

# The two tiers, and what a traveller knows

A route is planned on two tiers, the split [navigation]({{< ref "server/ai" >}}) already makes:

- **Coarse:** the macro nav graph of roads, bridges, gates and open country, generated by the
  `nav_graph` stage. A* over it gives a *course*: the nodes to reach and the edge to each.
- **Detailed:** the local A* over the walk data of the ground as it is now, 32 tiles at a time. Player
  edits change it at once.

The coarse planner does **not** read whether an edge is truly blocked. It reads what the traveller's
**knower** believes:

- The knower of a master and of all its bestias is the master. What any of them sees is known to all.
- The knower of an NPC is the NPC itself.

An edge nobody saw is believed open, as generated. So a bestia sent across a bridge that fell where nobody of
its master's saw it walks to that bridge. It turns back when the bridge comes into sight, and only then plans
the way round. This is `RouteKnowledge`.

A traveller learns in two ways:

| How                          | When                                                                                     | Believed for          |
|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| **By seeing** (`RouteSense`) | a road, bridge or gate comes within the 3×3 chunks around it, or changes while it is near | `knowledge-ttl`, 21 d |
| **By failing**               | it makes no headway on an edge for `stall-after`, 10 s - a wall across a mountain pass   | `failure-ttl`, 1 d    |

Seeing an edge standing again clears the belief. Only blockages are kept, and a master's are stored
(`route_blockage`), so they outlast a restart. Learning anything new makes every journey of that knower plan
again from where it stands.

# Vicinity: what an entity notices

`Vicinity` is the general "what is near me" layer, in `world/vicinity`, for anything later that needs an NPC
to sense its surroundings. An entity with a `Vicinity` covers the chunk columns within its radius.
`VicinitySystem` moves the cover only when the entity crosses a chunk border, and tells each
`VicinitySense` which columns just came near. `VicinityIndex` answers the other way round, who is near a
column, so a sense can push a change to exactly those entities. A standing entity costs one lookup per tick.

Every master and bestia has a `Vicinity` from the moment it spawns, so a broken bridge a player walks past by
hand is known to the master's bestias as well.

# The simulation around every bestia

A bestia away from any player used to stand in a frozen world: walk data existed only for chunks sent to a
client, and dens and wildlife only woke near the entity a player drives. So it could not path, and nothing
could find it.

- **`ChunkResidency`** refcounts which chunks are simulated. All clients together are one holder; walk data
  and ground marks exist for the held chunks. Props stay scoped to what clients see.
- Every master and player bestia is a **`SimulationAnchor`**. `AnchorResidencySystem` holds the same view
  volume around it that a client is offered, so it can path. Dens and wildlife wake around anchors, and AI
  near one runs at full rate. Townsfolk stay where clients look, because they need props.
- **Hostility:** wild creatures now see owned bestias as enemies, so they find a bestia out on its own.
  Owned bestias still do not hunt each other.
- A lone anchor holds about 180 chunks, some 2 MB of walk data. `simulation-anchors.max-holding-anchors`
  (512) caps how many hold ground: the driven entity first, then travellers, then resting bestias.

# The journey engine

`TravelSystem` runs before `MoveSystem` and carries each journey one step further:

1. A dead traveller ends the journey (`DIED`).
2. Reaching the current waypoint moves on to the next, or ends the journey as arrived.
3. Without a plan, or after the knower learned something, `RoutePlanner` plans the course. With no known
   way the journey ends (`NO_ROUTE`).
4. `LegRefiner` turns the next stretch into steps. It searches again when the leg runs short and every
   `repath-interval` (2 s) on the way, so a wall built in front of the traveller is walked round. A leg that
   already ends on its aim is left alone; `MoveSystem` refuses a blocked step anyway, and the leg is then
   searched again.
5. `ProgressWatchdog` watches the distance to the aim. Without headway it first blames the edge ahead
   (learning by failing). After `give-up-after` (30 s) the journey ends (`STUCK`): walled in, or in a dead
   end.

Searches come from their own budget (`travel.pathfinds-per-tick`), so travel cannot starve the AI's. The
journeys that waited longest go first.

**Player orders suspend the AI.** A bestia on a journey its player ordered keeps walking, attacked or not.
When the AI takes it back - after a journey, a switch to another entity or a logout - its home becomes where
it stands, so it does not walk back to where it used to live.

**AI orders hold a lease.** `TravelTo` renews it each time it is ticked. When the plan moves on, the leaf is
no longer ticked, the lease lapses, and the journey is dropped without a word.

# Messages

| Message              | Direction | What                                                                                           |
|----------------------|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `MoveEntity`         | in        | Any owned entity, waypoints (none stops it), `append`, the client's first leg, a route's name and id |
| `JourneyComponent`   | out       | Owner only, at any distance: waypoints, the next index, name and the way ahead in coarse points |
| `JourneyEnded`       | out       | To the owner: arrived, or `NO_ROUTE`, `STUCK`, `DIED`, and where                                |
| `SavedRoutes` (+4)   | both      | The master's routes, kept, renamed and forgotten by name                                         |

`MoveEntity` replaced `MoveActiveEntity`. Naming the entity grants nothing new: selecting another owned entity
and moving it was always open to a client. The server owns every step. The client still walks its own
click at once, and its first steps are walked as sent when each one is valid, so the prediction is not
corrected a round trip later.

A bestia's journey is kept in its row (`player_bestia.journey`), so it goes on after a restart.

# Saved routes

A **saved route** is a named list of waypoints a master keeps, at most 32. With no name given it is named from
the places it starts and ends in ("Ironhill → Ashford"); a blank rename gives that name back. A route saved
from the map starts where the entity stands, so it can be sent back the other way. To send one, the client
sends its waypoints itself with its name and id, so the tick never reads the database.
